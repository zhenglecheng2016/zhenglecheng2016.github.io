AI-Enabled Business Analytics & Decision-Making

I am a Presidential Postdoctoral Fellow at the Pamplin College of Business, Virginia Tech, working with Professors Jian Ni and Chris Zobel. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, advised by Professor Jingrui He.

My research integrates artificial intelligence, statistical inference, operations research, and economic modeling to understand how organizations can acquire, share, and learn from imperfect information to make reliable and economically valuable decisions.

Research agenda

How can organizations learn from imperfect information and translate that learning into reliable, economically valuable decisions?

My research addresses three connected challenges: acquiring reliable information, learning from fragmented information, and designing incentives for information sharing and decision-making.

01

Digital experimentation

How should firms allocate resources between experiments and validation when observed outcomes are unreliable?

02

Customer intelligence

When can firms detect coordinated behavior from fragmented customer networks without pooling raw records?

03

Information economics

How should incentives and regulatory policies govern information sharing and interventions across competing firms?

Substantive interests: Business intelligence, digital experimentation, customer analytics, economic networks, risk management, and information design.

Methodological interests: Machine learning and AI for business, graph and multimodal learning, adaptive experimentation, measurement error, sequential decision-making, and mechanism design.

Working papers

Research on experimentation, information sharing, and risk analytics in business and economic systems.

Digital experimentation

Test the Creative, Audit the Measurement: Adaptive Validation Allocation for Large-Scale Creative Experiments

Job market paper · Work in progress

Lecheng Zheng, Jiyuan Yin, Jian Ni

Develops theoretical allocation rules and an adaptive experimental design to balance advertising exposure and costly outcome validation when measurement error differs across advertisements. Theoretical development is in place; empirical evaluation is planned.

Information economics

Compliance Moral Hazard and the Backfiring Mandate

Management Science · Major revision · Paper

Jian Ni, Lecheng Zheng, John R. Birge

Studies incentive-compatible information sharing and the welfare effects of regulatory mandates in competitive markets. Shows how poorly designed mandates can backfire under the model’s conditions.

Customer intelligence

Detecting Coordinated Behavior in Fragmented Networks: A Cross-Entity Diffusion Approach to Customer Risk Analytics

Preparing for submission to Operations Research

Lecheng Zheng, Jian Ni, Chris Zobel, John R. Birge

Establishes conditions under which cross-entity diffusion can recover coordinated customer groups without pooling raw records, emphasizing seed quality, dispersion, and learned edge-score contrast.
